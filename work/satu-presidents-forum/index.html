International programmes

Running the secretariat for an international university forum

As project manager at NCKU's Office of International Affairs, I ran the international secretariat for the SATU Presidents' Forum — and treated it as an ongoing programme, not a once-a-year event.

Project manager 2019 – 2020 Office of International Affairs National Cheng Kung University

Context

The SATU Presidents' Forum connects universities across Southeast and South Asia and Taiwan. National Cheng Kung University hosts its international secretariat, and my role sat at the centre of that — the coordinating point between member universities, our own leadership, and the events that keep the network active.

Problem

International collaboration lives or dies on follow-through. Member universities are busy, spread across time zones and languages, and a forum only creates value if the conversations turn into real academic collaboration and student mobility afterwards — not applause on the day and silence for the rest of the year.

That mattered because the secretariat is NCKU's standing commitment to the network. If coordination slipped, so did the university's relationships across an entire region.

What I did

I treated the secretariat as a year-round programme with the forum as one milestone, and put the coordinating work where the value actually accrued — between events.

  • Ran the secretariat's day-to-day coordination between member universities and NCKU leadership.
  • Managed international events end-to-end — planning, logistics, and on-the-day facilitation.
  • Carried academic collaboration and student-mobility initiatives forward after each forum, rather than letting them lapse.
  • Communicated across languages and cultures to keep partners engaged in the quiet months between events.

The measure I trusted was continuity: partners staying in the conversation and collaborations moving forward between forums, not just seats filled on the day.

Trade-offs and what I chose not to do

The constant tension was depth versus reach. A regional network wants everyone included; real collaboration comes from a few committed relationships. I resolved it by putting my energy where reciprocity was already there.

  • Chose not to chase every possible member equally — I prioritised active, reciprocal relationships.
  • Chose not to treat the forum as a standalone event — the follow-up mattered more than the day itself.

The cost we accepted

Focusing on committed partners meant slower breadth. I judged a smaller number of real, moving collaborations to be worth more than a long list of names on a membership page.

Outcome

The forum ran as a coordinated programme rather than a one-off, with the secretariat keeping partners engaged and collaboration and student-mobility conversations moving between events. I left the role in 2020 — but the relationships and the coordination habits were built to outlast any single forum, which is the point of a secretariat.

Role

What I'd do differently

I'd have built lighter, shared tracking with member universities earlier, so follow-through didn't rest so heavily on the secretariat chasing each thread by hand. The relationships were strong; the systems around them could have carried more of the weight.
